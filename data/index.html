Sadeem Open Data

Sadeem's reference table, used to add your own objects to the app, is built from two open catalogues published under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International licence (CC BY-SA 4.0):

  • OpenNGC by Mattia Verga — https://github.com/mattiaverga/OpenNGC
  • HYG Stellar Database 4.4 by David Nash (astronexus) — https://codeberg.org/astronexus/hyg

Beside the table, the free Sky Map uses deep-stars.bin: the stars of the HYG Stellar Database 4.4 by David Nash (astronexus) from magnitude 6.5 to 9 that are not in the table, shown as you zoom in. It is adapted from HYG, which is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Our adapted table and our adapted deep-stars.bin are available here under the same licence, CC BY-SA 4.0. You may share and adapt them, including commercially, as long as you credit the original authors and Sadeem, and share your adaptations under the same licence.

Files

  • reference-objects.json — the adapted table (CC BY-SA 4.0)
  • deep-stars.bin — HYG stars from magnitude 6.5 to 9 not in the table above, packed as binary records (layout in reference_tables.py, deep_star_bytes); adapted by Sadeem, CC BY-SA 4.0

What we changed

The table converts both catalogues into one compact file and trims them:

  • stars come from HYG: every star brighter than magnitude 6.5 and every star with a proper name;
  • each star also carries HYG's B−V colour index (the ci column), used to tint it on the map;
  • deep-sky objects come from OpenNGC; its star, asterism, association, duplicate, group, non-existent and nova entries are left out, and duplicates become aliases of the object they repeat;
  • coordinates are converted to decimal degrees, and each row records which of Sadeem's own objects it is, if any.

deep-stars.bin keeps only HYG's stars fainter than magnitude 6.5, down to 9, that have no proper name, and only four things for each, in ten bytes: right ascension and declination in degrees (little-endian 32-bit floats), the magnitude above 6.5 in hundredths (one unsigned byte), and the B−V colour index in fiftieths (one signed byte, −128 where it is unknown).
